Corrugate Sheet Sourcing: Antidumping Duties and True Landed Cost

Corrugatesheet Editorial • November 2026

Unit Price Is Not Landed Cost

When procurement teams compare PP corrugate sheet quotes, the trap is to anchor on the ex-works unit price. For US buyers, the number that actually hits the budget is landed cost: unit price plus ocean freight, insurance, customs duty, antidumping duty where applicable, brokerage, and inland transport to the dock. Two quotes with identical unit prices can diverge sharply once duty exposure is factored in, and antidumping duty is the single largest swing variable for corrugated plastic sourced from certain origins.

How Antidumping Duties Work

Antidumping duties (ADD) are levied when imported goods are found to be sold below fair market value, and they are assessed on top of ordinary customs duty. For corrugated plastic and related PP products, ADD rates tied to specific countries of origin can add a substantial percentage to landed cost, in some cases large enough to erase the apparent savings of a low unit price. Critically, ADD is origin-specific: the same product manufactured in a country not subject to the order carries no antidumping exposure at all.

  • Customs duty: the standard tariff for the product classification
  • Antidumping duty: an additional origin-specific levy where an order applies
  • Freight and insurance: ocean and inland transport plus coverage
  • Brokerage and fees: customs clearance and handling charges

The Origin Advantage

Because ADD is tied to country of origin, sourcing strategy can neutralize it entirely. Manufacturing in Thailand and Vietnam, for example, falls outside the antidumping orders that affect other origins, meaning US buyers pay 0% ADD on qualifying corrugate sheet from those countries. This is not a loophole; it is a legitimate consequence of where the goods are produced, and it can shift total landed cost by a double-digit percentage on large programs. Buyers evaluating PP corrugated boxes and matching sheet stock should confirm the manufacturing origin up front and factor the duty profile into every quote comparison.

Direct Manufacturer vs Distributor Markup

Landed cost is also shaped by how many intermediaries sit between the factory and the dock. Each distributor layer adds markup, and some repackagers obscure the true country of origin, leaving the importer exposed to duty surprises at clearance. Buying from a direct manufacturer with transparent origin documentation removes those markups and gives the procurement team a clean, auditable duty position. It also opens access to custom sizing, so the team can order exactly the sheet dimensions and gauge they need through custom corrugated plastic sheets rather than paying for stock sizes that waste material.

Building a Landed-Cost Model

A disciplined sourcing decision rests on a landed-cost model that captures every line. Start with the ex-works unit price, add ocean freight and insurance to the US port, apply the correct customs duty rate for the tariff classification, add any antidumping duty for the specific origin, and finish with brokerage and inland freight to the facility. Running this model across candidate origins frequently reveals that a higher ex-works price from a 0% ADD country beats a lower price from an origin carrying a heavy antidumping levy.

De-Risking the Supply Chain

Beyond the arithmetic, origin diversity reduces risk. Concentrating all volume in a single country exposes the program to policy changes, port disruptions, and new trade actions. A manufacturer with production in multiple Southeast Asian countries, and US manufacturing in development, gives buyers continuity options and a path toward shorter domestic lead times as that capacity comes online. For procurement teams planning multi-year corrugate programs, that roadmap is a meaningful part of the sourcing decision.

Conclusion

For US buyers of PP corrugate sheets, landed cost, not unit price, is the number that matters, and antidumping duty is the variable most likely to upend a naive comparison. By sourcing from 0% ADD origins such as Thailand and Vietnam, buying direct to strip out distributor markup, and modeling every landed-cost line, procurement teams secure lower total cost and a cleaner, lower-risk duty position across the whole program.
