FDA & USDA Compliance Guide for PP Corrugate Sheet Applications

Corrugatesheet Editorial • September 2026

Navigating Compliance Requirements for Food-Contact PP Corrugate

Polypropylene corrugated sheet is widely used in food processing, produce handling, and cold-chain logistics. Its durability, washability, and moisture resistance make it a natural fit for regulated environments—but procurement and operations teams in FDA-inspected or USDA-regulated facilities must verify that their PP corrugate meets applicable compliance requirements before bringing it into direct or indirect food contact.

This guide covers the key FDA and USDA frameworks relevant to PP corrugate sheet applications, what documentation to request from suppliers, and how to structure your compliance program for audit readiness.

FDA Regulation of Food-Contact Plastics

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration regulates materials that come into direct or indirect contact with food under 21 CFR (Code of Federal Regulations). For polypropylene, the relevant reference is 21 CFR 177.1520, which establishes conditions under which olefin polymers—including polypropylene—may be safely used as food-contact articles.

Key compliance considerations under 21 CFR 177.1520 include:

  • Resin must be of the specified polymer type—PP homopolymer or copolymer, as applicable to the finished article
  • Additives, colorants, and processing aids must comply with applicable FDA regulations or carry GRAS (Generally Recognized As Safe) status
  • The finished article must meet extractable limits if it contacts aqueous, acidic, or fatty food products under intended use conditions

For PP corrugate sheets used as produce packaging, layer pads, or slip sheets in food-grade facilities, procurement teams should request written confirmation from the supplier that resin and additives comply with 21 CFR 177.1520. Do not rely on informal assurances—obtain documentation that can be filed and produced during regulatory inspection.

USDA Considerations for Meat, Poultry, and Egg Product Facilities

USDA-inspected facilities operating under FSIS (Food Safety and Inspection Service) oversight have additional compliance obligations for packaging and materials used in production areas. For PP corrugate used in these environments:

  • Sanitation compliance: Reusable packaging materials in USDA-inspected facilities must be included in the facility's SSOP (Sanitation Standard Operating Procedures). PP corrugate must be demonstrated as cleanable and sanitizable in a manner meeting FSIS sanitation requirements for the specific use environment.
  • Material suitability: FSIS does not publish a specific approved materials list equivalent to FDA's CFR references, but inspectors evaluate whether materials are safe, non-toxic, and non-absorbent. PP corrugate's closed-cell, non-porous structure directly supports this determination.
  • HACCP integration: If PP corrugate sheets are used in a production area covered by a HACCP plan, the cleaning and sanitation of those sheets should be addressed in the facility's HACCP documentation or associated prerequisite programs.

Sourcing Compliant PP Corrugate: What to Request

When evaluating suppliers of PP corrugate sheets for food-contact applications, request the following documentation as part of your supplier qualification process:

  • FDA compliance letter or declaration: Supplier confirmation that resin meets 21 CFR 177.1520 requirements for the intended food-contact application
  • Material safety data sheet (SDS/MSDS): Identifies resin composition, additives, and any components relevant to safety evaluation
  • Resin certification: Mill certification or resin supplier documentation identifying the polymer type, grade, and compliance status
  • Colorant compliance (if applicable): Confirmation that colorants or dyes used in non-natural sheets are FDA-compliant for the intended food-contact category

These documents form the basis of your supplier qualification record and should be maintained on file and accessible for regulatory audit or customer audit purposes.

Custom Specifications and Compliance Consistency

Food processors and distributors frequently require custom dimensions, thicknesses, or surface finishes for PP corrugate sheet applications. It is essential that compliance documentation applies specifically to the material as produced—not generically to PP as a polymer class.

When ordering custom corrugated plastic sheets, confirm with the supplier that the compliance declaration covers the specific resin lot and additive package used in your production order. Batch-to-batch consistency in resin sourcing is a reasonable expectation to formalize in supplier agreements for regulated applications, and reputable manufacturers will accommodate this in their quality documentation.

Audit Preparation Checklist

For facilities subject to FDA inspections under FSMA or otherwise, or USDA/FSIS audits, the following checklist covers PP corrugate sheet compliance documentation requirements:

  • Supplier FDA compliance letter on file, referencing 21 CFR 177.1520
  • Resin SDS/MSDS on file and current, reviewed annually or upon supplier change
  • Colorant compliance documentation on file if non-natural color sheets are in use
  • Sanitation protocol documented in SSOP or HACCP prerequisite programs
  • Cleaning agent compatibility confirmed—no agents leaving residues incompatible with food contact
  • Inspection and retirement criteria documented and communicated to operations staff
  • Supplier qualification record established and maintained with date of last review
